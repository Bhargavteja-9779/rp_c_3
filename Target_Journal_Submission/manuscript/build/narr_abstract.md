Time series classifiers used in monitoring systems, from heart monitors to industrial sensors, often decide on their own and pass uncertain cases to a person. This only works if their confidence drops when a recording is damaged by noise, drift, spikes or dropouts. Fast random convolutional kernel classifiers such as MiniRocket are accurate and cheap to train, but their ridge readout produces scores rather than probabilities, and existing calibration methods rescale all scores in the same way, so faulty recordings still receive confident answers. We propose LOOM, a closed-form readout that divides each ridge score by the square root of its leverage, a measure of how far a recording lies from the training data, and fits a single temperature and the ridge penalty on exact leave-one-out pairs of scores and leverages. LOOM needs no held-out data, no refitting and no ensemble, and the same identities give exact jackknife+ prediction sets. On {{S['n_datasets']}} UCR datasets with 18 simulated sensor faults, LOOM matched the best existing readout on clean data and lowered the median negative log-likelihood under faults from {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal'))}} to {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','LOOM'))}} (better on {{pw('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal')['ref_wins']}} of {{pw('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal')['n']}} datasets). In a human-in-the-loop study on cardiac, wearable and industrial sensor data with 20 % of recordings sent to review, its review queue caught {{pct(cs('stream:fault_caught@20','LOOM'),0)}} of the faulty recordings, against {{pct(cs('stream:fault_caught@20','PreVal'),0)}} for the best previous readout. LOOM {{quant_phrase()}} and does not detect changes that leave the features within the range of the training data. Code and results are publicly available.
