\textbf{Submission of an original research article: ``Knowing when to ask a human: fault-aware confidence for fast time series classifiers''}

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

\textbf{The practical problem.} Monitoring systems for heart signals, wearables and industrial sensors increasingly let a classifier decide on its own and pass uncertain recordings to a person. This only works if the classifier's confidence drops when a recording is damaged by noise, drift, spikes or dropouts. The fastest accurate time series classifiers, random convolutional kernel methods such as MiniRocket, do not have such a confidence: their existing probability outputs rescale all scores in the same way.

\textbf{What the paper offers.} We propose LOOM, a closed-form readout that scales each prediction by the ridge leverage of the recording, a measure of how far it lies from the training data, and calibrates it on exact leave-one-out quantities without held-out data, refitting or ensembles. We also release an open-source command-line tool that turns labelled recordings into a triage system with a review queue.

\textbf{The evidence.} On \NDatasets{} UCR datasets with 18 simulated sensor faults, LOOM lowered the median negative log-likelihood from \NLLFaultsPreVal{} to \NLLFaultsLoom{} compared with the strongest previous readout (PreVal). Under real recorded ECG noise from the MIT-BIH Noise Stress Test Database it lowered it from \RNNLLPreVal{} to \RNNLLLoom. In a pre-registered triage study on \CNDatasets{} sensor datasets, its review queue caught \CCaughtLoom{} of the faulty recordings with a 20\,\% review budget, against \CCaughtPreVal{} for PreVal. \NHypPass{} of \NHyp{} pre-specified hypotheses passed. All numbers are generated by one script from released result files.

\textbf{Fit with the journal.} The work addresses machine learning, data mining and pattern recognition for sensor data, which are within the journal's scope, and offers a computationally light solution for monitoring applications where data are scarce and decisions are shared with a human reviewer.

\textbf{Main limitations, stated in the paper.} On clean data LOOM is not better than PreVal and its calibration error is higher; the tree-based QUANT classifier has a lower likelihood loss under the strongest faults; leverage does not detect changes that keep the features in the training range; apart from the real ECG noise, the faults are simulated.
