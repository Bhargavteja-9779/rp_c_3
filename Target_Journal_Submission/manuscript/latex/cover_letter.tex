\textbf{Submission of a full-length article: ``Can a fast time series classifier tell when a sensor recording is faulty? Leverage-aware confidence and its limits''}

Dear Editors,

\textbf{The practical problem.} Decision-support systems for heart signals, wearables and industrial sensors often let a classifier decide alone and pass uncertain recordings to a person. This only works if the classifier's confidence drops when a recording is damaged. The fastest accurate time series classifiers, random convolutional kernel methods such as MiniRocket, have no such confidence: their probability outputs rescale all scores in the same way.

\textbf{What the paper offers.} We propose LOOM, a closed-form readout that scales each prediction by the ridge leverage of the recording, a measure of how far it lies from the training data, calibrated on exact leave-one-out quantities without held-out data, refitting or ensembles. We release an open-source command-line tool that turns labelled recordings into a triage system with a review queue.

\textbf{The evidence, positive and negative.} All hypotheses were registered before the experiments that test them. With strong artefacts LOOM helps: under 18 simulated faults on \NDatasets{} UCR datasets it lowered the median log-loss from \NLLFaultsPreVal{} to \NLLFaultsLoom{} compared with the strongest previous readout, and in a confirmatory triage study on \CNDatasets{} datasets its review queue caught \CCaughtLoom{} of the faulty recordings, against \CCaughtPreVal. On real data from another acquisition setting, \CincN{} 12-lead ECGs graded by annotators in the PhysioNet/Computing in Cardiology Challenge 2011, models trained on PTB-XL and applied unchanged put \CincHTwelveCaughtLoom{} of the unusable recordings into a 20\,\% review queue, against \CincHTwelveCaughtPreVal{} for PreVal and \CincCaughtDeepEns{} for a deep ensemble. We also report what tempers this: a simple amplitude rule catches many of the same recordings, and the leverage on its own is a better quality score than LOOM's confidence. On the milder expert-annotated noise of \PTBN{} clinical ECGs from PTB-XL, LOOM helped at most marginally, and no method we tested, deep ensembles and Monte Carlo dropout included, did much better than chance. \NHypPass{} of \NHyp{} pre-specified hypotheses passed; the failures are reported in full and define where the method can be trusted.

\textbf{Fit with the journal.} The paper concerns an intelligent decision-support component (when should an automatic system hand a case to a human expert?), evaluated on real applications, with a validated open-source tool. It positions itself among recent \emph{Expert Systems with Applications} work on confidence-based deferral and routing and on MiniRocket-based fault diagnosis, and compares with deep uncertainty baselines. We think its practical message---that triage systems must be validated on real annotated faults, that a cheap leverage-based confidence catches gross faults that accurate deep models miss, and that the review queue should come from the model that makes the decisions---is useful to readers who build such systems.
