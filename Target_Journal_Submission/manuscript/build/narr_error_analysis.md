**Where the gain comes from.** Across datasets, the NLL improvement of LOOM over PreVal under corruption is strongly correlated with how much the corruptions increase leverage (Spearman ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift','p'))}}, n = {{corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift','n')}}), which supports the intended mechanism. The share of datasets on which LOOM has the lower NLL grows with severity for every family except time warping. At severity 3 it is {{pct(winrate('noise',3),0)}} for noise, {{pct(winrate('spikes',3),0)}} for spikes, {{pct(winrate('missing',3),0)}} for missing segments, {{pct(winrate('wander',3),0)}} for wander and {{pct(winrate('shift',3),0)}} for circular shifts, but only {{pct(winrate('warp',3),0)}} for time warping, whose median leverage ratio stays at {{f2(lev('warp3'))}}. Smooth warps of up to ±20 % leave the pooled MiniRocket features largely inside the span of the training features: leverage then carries no signal and LOOM behaves like a temperature-scaled ridge. At severity 1 the win rates are {{pct(winrate('noise',1),0)}}–{{pct(winrate('missing',1),0)}} (noise, missing), i.e. mild corruptions are not reliably distinguished from in-distribution variation.

**Failure modes.** (i) *Over-moderation of shifted but still correct predictions.* The datasets on which LOOM loses most to PreVal under corruption ({{worst('shift',0)}}, {{worst('shift',1)}}, {{worst('shift',2)}}) keep comparatively high accuracy under corruption (e.g. {{f2(worst('shift',2,'acc_shift'))}} for {{worst('shift',2)}}) while their leverage grows ({{f1(worst('shift',2,'lev_ratio_shift'))}}×), so lowering confidence costs likelihood. Leverage measures distance from the training data, not the probability of error; across datasets the gain under corruption is negatively correlated with the accuracy retained under corruption (ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_shift~acc_shift'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_shift~acc_shift','p'))}}). (ii) *Many classes with very few examples per class.* On clean data the gain over PreVal is negatively correlated with the number of classes (ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_clean~n_classes'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_clean~n_classes','p'))}}). The largest clean losses occur on {{worst('clean',0)}}, {{worst('clean',2)}} and {{worst('clean',3)}} (52 or 60 classes with about two to ten training series per class), where Platt smoothing assigns each true class only (n_k + 1)/(n_k + 2) of the target mass and the fitted model becomes underconfident. This is also the main source of LOOM's higher in-distribution ECE (Section 6.1).
