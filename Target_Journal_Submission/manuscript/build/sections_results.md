## 6.4 Ablation (RQ2)

Fig. 7 shows the median NLL difference of each variant to LOOM (variant − LOOM; positive values favour LOOM). **Leverage moderation.** Removing it while keeping target smoothing (PreVal-smooth) is {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','PreVal-smooth')}} under corruption (median NLL {{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','PreVal-smooth'))}} vs {{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM'))}}; {{dd('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','PreVal-smooth')}}). On clean data it is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','PreVal-smooth')}} ({{dd('E3_ablation_clean','nll','PreVal-smooth')}}; PreVal-smooth is lower on {{pw('E3_ablation_clean','nll','PreVal-smooth')['ref_losses']}} of 114 datasets), so the moderation costs at most a small amount of in-distribution NLL. **Exact LOO pairs.** Fitting the calibrator on in-sample pairs is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-insample')}} on clean data (median NLL {{f(med('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-insample'))}}), and an 80/20 hold-out split is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-holdout')}} (median {{f(med('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-holdout'))}}), because the ridge is then trained on fewer series; under corruption they are {{both(varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-insample'), varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-holdout'))}} too. **Target smoothing.** Without it LOOM is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-nosmooth')}} on clean NLL and {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-nosmooth')}} under corruption ({{dd('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-nosmooth')}}). **Moderation strength.** Learning a free strength b on the LOO pairs (LOOM-free-b) and the finite strength c = 1 are {{both(varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-free-b'), varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-finite-c1'))}} on clean data ({{dd('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-free-b')}} and {{dd('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}}). Under corruption LOOM-free-b is {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-free-b')}} (median NLL {{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-free-b'))}}; {{dd('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-free-b')}}), presumably because the held-out leverages vary little, so b is poorly identified (as observed during development), and c = 1 is {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}} ({{dd('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}}). For shift detection, however, LOOM's AUROC ({{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM'))}}) exceeds that of c = 1 ({{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM-finite-c1'))}}; LOOM higher on {{pw('E3_ablation_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM-finite-c1')['ref_wins']}} of 114 datasets) and of LOOM-free-b ({{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM-free-b'))}}), and the k-NN distance also detects shift worse ({{f(med('E3_ablation_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM-kNN'))}}). The pre-registered limit c → ∞ therefore trades a very small clean-data NLL loss for better shift detection. **Other choices.** Replacing leverage by a k-NN distance is {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-kNN')}} in NLL under corruption, and choosing λ by LOO squared error is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-lambdaSSE')}} on clean data.

## 6.5 Generalisation (RQ4)

**Other random-kernel extractors.** With MultiRocket features (official split, clean and severity-3 conditions), LOOM is {{cmp('E5_multirocket_shifted','nll','PreVal')}} under corruption and {{cmp('E5_multirocket_clean','nll','PreVal')}} on clean data. With Hydra features the corresponding comparisons are {{cmp('E5_hydra_shifted','nll','PreVal')}} and {{cmp('E5_hydra_clean','nll','PreVal')}}.

**Subsets.** On the {{S['n_unseen']}} datasets never used during development LOOM is {{cmp('sens_unseen_shifted','nll','PreVal')}} under corruption and {{cmp('sens_unseen_clean','nll','PreVal')}} on clean data. Excluding the 16 datasets with train/test duplicates ({{S['n_nodup']}} datasets), the comparisons are {{cmp('sens_nodup_shifted','nll','PreVal')}} and {{cmp('sens_nodup_clean','nll','PreVal')}}.

**Related datasets.** Several UCR datasets share a data source (e.g. the four UWaveGestureLibrary sets or the six phalanx-outline sets), so datasets are not fully independent units. Averaging related datasets into {{S['sens_grouped_shifted']['nll']['n_units']}} source-level units, LOOM has the lower corrupted-data NLL than PreVal for {{pwg('shifted','nll','PreVal')['ref_wins']}} of {{pwg('shifted','nll','PreVal')['n']}} units (Holm-adjusted p {{pv(pwg('shifted','nll','PreVal')['p_holm'])}}), while on clean data the comparison remains {{'not significant' if pwg('clean','nll','PreVal')['p_holm'] >= 0.05 else 'significant'}} ({{pwg('clean','nll','PreVal')['ref_wins']}}/{{pwg('clean','nll','PreVal')['ref_losses']}} wins/losses, Holm-adjusted p {{pv(pwg('clean','nll','PreVal')['p_holm'])}}).

**A natural sensor shift.** To complement the synthetic corruptions we use the three accelerometer axes of UWaveGestureLibrary, which contain the same {{ds_total('UWaveGestureLibraryX')}} gesture recordings with identical labels. A model trained on one axis is tested on the same recordings measured on another axis ({{S['E10_natural_shift']['n_tasks']}} ordered axis pairs × {{S['E10_natural_shift']['n_resamples']}} resamples; the same split seed keeps test recordings disjoint from training recordings). This shift is severe (mean accuracy drops from {{f(ns('LOOM','acc_in'))}} to {{f(ns('LOOM','acc_shift'))}}) but barely changes leverage: the median ratio h(x)/h_ref is only {{f2(nslev()[0])}}–{{f2(nslev()[1])}}, because the other axes produce MiniRocket features inside the span of the training features. Correspondingly, LOOM's advantage is small. Its mean NLL on the shifted axis is {{f(ns('LOOM','nll_shift'))}} against {{f(ns('PreVal','nll_shift'))}} for PreVal (lower on {{nsw('nll_shift','PreVal')['ref_wins']}} of {{nsw('nll_shift','PreVal')['n']}} tasks), and its shift-detection AUROC is {{f(ns('LOOM','shift_auroc'))}} against {{f(ns('PreVal','shift_auroc'))}}. The uncalibrated temperature-1 softmax, which is underconfident everywhere, attains the lowest NLL under this shift ({{f(ns('Ridge-softmax','nll_shift'))}}), but it is far worse in-domain ({{f(ns('Ridge-softmax','nll_in'))}} vs {{f(ns('LOOM','nll_in'))}}). Leverage moderation therefore protects against shifts that move inputs away from the training feature span, not against every distribution shift (Section 8).

## 6.8 Sensitivity (RQ6)

Compared with the pre-registered limit c → ∞, the finite value c = 1 is {{varword('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}} on clean data ({{dd('E3_ablation_clean','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}}) and {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}} under corruption ({{dd('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-finite-c1')}}), but its shift-detection AUROC is lower (Section 6.4). For conformal sets, the clean coverage of LOOM's jackknife+ is {{f(cp('CP-LOOM-jackknife+@0.05'))}} at α = 0.05 and {{f(cp('CP-LOOM-jackknife+@0.2'))}} at α = 0.2. **Tuning of the logistic-regression baseline.** In a check on 12 randomly chosen datasets (official split; Online Resource 3), training LogReg-CV with 3,000 instead of 300 iterations and tolerance 10⁻⁵ instead of 10⁻³ changed its median NLL from {{f(LR['med_def'],4)}} to {{f(LR['med_long'],4)}}, but improved it by {{f(LR['max_gain'])}} on {{LR['max_ds']}}, where the selected C moved to the upper end of the grid. The clean-data comparison with LogReg-CV should therefore be read as approximate for datasets with many classes. With 25 % and 50 % of the training data (official split, clean and severity-3 conditions; Online Resource 3) LOOM versus PreVal gives, on clean data, {{tsp(0.25,'clean')}} at 25 % and {{tsp(0.5,'clean')}} at 50 %; under severity-3 corruption, {{tsp(0.25,'shifted')}} at 25 % and {{tsp(0.5,'shifted')}} at 50 % (Table S4). {{tsclaim()}}
