**Where the gain comes from.** Across datasets, the NLL improvement of LOOM over PreVal under corruption is positively correlated with how much the corruptions increase leverage (Spearman ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift','p'))}}, n = {{corr('gain_shift~lev_ratio_shift','n')}}), which supports the intended mechanism. The share of datasets on which LOOM has the lower NLL {{trendclaim()}}. At severity 3 it is {{pct(winrate('noise',3),0)}} for noise, {{pct(winrate('spikes',3),0)}} for spikes, {{pct(winrate('missing',3),0)}} for missing segments, {{pct(winrate('wander',3),0)}} for wander and {{pct(winrate('shift',3),0)}} for circular shifts, but only {{pct(winrate('warp',3),0)}} for time warping, whose median leverage ratio stays at {{f2(lev('warp3'))}}. Smooth warps of up to ±20 % leave the pooled MiniRocket features largely inside the span of the training features: leverage then carries no signal and LOOM behaves like a temperature-scaled ridge. At severity 1 the win rates of the other families are only {{sev1range()}}, i.e. mild corruptions are not reliably distinguished from in-distribution variation.

**Failure modes.** (i) *Over-moderation of shifted but still correct predictions.* The datasets on which LOOM loses most to PreVal under corruption ({{worst('shift',0)}}, {{worst('shift',1)}}, {{worst('shift',2)}}) keep comparatively high accuracy under corruption (e.g. {{f2(worst('shift',2,'acc_shift'))}} for {{worst('shift',2)}}) while their leverage grows ({{f1(worst('shift',2,'lev_ratio_shift'))}}×), so lowering confidence costs likelihood. Leverage measures distance from the training data, not the probability of error; across datasets the gain under corruption is negatively correlated with the accuracy retained under corruption (ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_shift~acc_shift'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_shift~acc_shift','p'))}}). (ii) *Many classes with very few examples per class.* On clean data the gain over PreVal is negatively correlated with the number of classes (ρ = {{f2(corr('gain_clean~n_classes'))}}, p {{pv(corr('gain_clean~n_classes','p'))}}). The four largest clean losses occur on {{worstlist('clean',4)}}, which have only {{perclass_range('clean',4)}} training series per class on average. With so few series per class, Platt smoothing assigns each true class only (n_k + 1)/(n_k + 2) of the target mass, and the fitted model becomes underconfident. Smoothing is also what raises LOOM's in-distribution ECE (Section 6.1): without it, the median clean ECE is {{f(med('E3_ablation_clean','ece','LOOM-nosmooth'))}}, at the level of PreVal ({{f(med('E3_ablation_clean','ece','PreVal'))}}), but the NLL under corruption is {{varword('E3_ablation_shifted','nll','LOOM-nosmooth')}} (Section 6.4).
