Monitoring systems, from heart monitors to industrial sensors, often let a time series classifier decide on its own and pass uncertain cases to a person. This only works if the classifier's confidence drops when a recording is damaged by noise, drift, spikes or dropouts. Fast random convolutional kernel classifiers such as MiniRocket are accurate and cheap, but their ridge readout gives scores rather than probabilities, and existing calibration methods rescale all scores in the same way, so faulty recordings still get confident answers. We propose LOOM, a closed-form readout that divides each ridge score by the square root of its leverage, which measures how far a recording lies from the training data, and fits one temperature and the ridge penalty on exact leave-one-out pairs of scores and leverages. LOOM needs no held-out data, refitting or ensemble, and also yields exact jackknife+ prediction sets. On {{S['n_datasets']}} UCR datasets with 18 simulated sensor faults, LOOM matched the best existing readout on clean data and lowered the median negative log-likelihood under faults from {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal'))}} to {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','LOOM'))}}. In a human-in-the-loop study on cardiac, wearable and industrial sensor data with a 20 % review budget, its review queue caught {{pct(cs('stream:fault_caught@20','LOOM'),0)}} of the faulty recordings, against {{pct(cs('stream:fault_caught@20','PreVal'),0)}} for the previous best readout and {{pct(cs('stream:fault_caught@20','QUANT'),0)}} for the tree-based QUANT classifier.
