Random convolutional kernel classifiers such as MiniRocket are accurate and fast, but their ridge readout returns scores rather than probabilities, and existing calibrations apply one global transformation that remains confident when test data drift. We propose LOOM, a closed-form probabilistic readout that divides each ridge logit by the square root of its leverage, the predictive variance factor of the equivalent Bayesian linear model, and fits a single temperature and the ridge penalty on exact leave-one-out pairs of scores and held-out leverages. LOOM needs one thin singular value decomposition, no held-out data, no refitting and no ensembles. The same identities yield exact jackknife+ prediction sets without refitting. On {{S['n_datasets']}} UCR datasets with five resamples and a pre-registered benchmark of 18 test-time corruptions, LOOM matches the strongest closed-form baseline on clean data in negative log-likelihood (NLL), although target smoothing raises its expected calibration error. Under corruption it lowers the median NLL from {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal'))}} to {{f2(med('E4_shifted','nll','LOOM'))}}, is better on {{pw('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal')['ref_wins']}} of {{pw('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal')['n']}} datasets (Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon p {{pv(pw('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal')['p_holm'])}}), {{'is on par with' if pw('E4_shifted','nll','QUANT')['p_holm']>=0.05 else ('better than' if pw('E4_shifted','nll','QUANT')['median_diff']>0 else 'worse than')}} the tree-based QUANT classifier, and raises the AUROC for detecting corrupted inputs from {{f2(med('E4_shifted','shift_auroc','PreVal'))}} to {{f2(med('E4_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM'))}}. Under a natural cross-axis sensor shift that barely changes leverage, the gain is small. Its clean jackknife+ sets reach {{f2(cp('CP-LOOM-jackknife+'))}} coverage at a nominal 0.90 with smaller sets than split conformal prediction. Code and all results are publicly available.
