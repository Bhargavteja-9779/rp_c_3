We proposed LOOM, a closed-form probabilistic readout for random convolutional kernel time series classifiers. It divides ridge logits by the square root of their leverage and fits one temperature and the ridge penalty on exact leave-one-out score–leverage pairs, so it needs no held-out data, refitting or ensembles. The same identities give exact jackknife+ prediction sets. On {{S['n_datasets']}} UCR datasets with five resamples, LOOM matches the best existing closed-form readout (PreVal) in-distribution in NLL, at the price of a higher ECE caused by target smoothing. Under 18 test-time corruptions it reduces the median NLL from {{f(med('E4_shifted','nll','PreVal'))}} to {{f(med('E4_shifted','nll','LOOM'))}}, better than every other readout, ensemble and raw-series classifier evaluated, and its confidence detects corrupted inputs much better (median AUROC {{f(med('E4_shifted','shift_auroc','LOOM'))}} vs {{f(med('E4_shifted','shift_auroc','PreVal'))}}). The fit costs essentially the same as the ridge readout itself. Future work includes multivariate series, real deployment shifts, adaptive smoothing for many-class few-shot problems, and moderation signals that also respond to shifts that leave the feature span unchanged.
